# V20.1 — Permanent QR identity: minimal architecture

Date: 2026-11-26. Recommended design direction for review; not production implementation approval.
References: [audit](v20.1-qr-architecture-review.md), [owner-approved product principle](../specifications/take-app-parity.md).

## Decision proposed
One logical Fixprio identity authority, one scanner, one existing Label Studio. Implement it as a small module with a durable store and a resolver, not a new microservice, separate staff application, or extra dashboard. Physical database placement remains open pending actual integration architecture. Redfix owns products, variants, stock and purchases. Revio owns repair tickets and their lifecycle. The identity authority owns the immutable token-to-domain-record mapping and its lifecycle; it does not own stock or repair status.

## Minimum model
- Permanent, opaque, randomly generated identifier with a versioned Fixprio namespace; unique constraint, collision handling, no reuse. Exact wire format is an implementation decision. No customer details, IMEI, price or mutable SKU in the payload. A code identifies a record, never grants access.
- Identity target: sellable product or exact variant, tracked individual unit when applicable, or repair ticket. Each target has a stable domain key. Existing manufacturer barcodes and SKUs are lookup aliases, not the permanent identifier. Preserve old issued QR mappings during migration; do not invalidate printed labels.
- Alias matching: normalize only according to the supported code format, retain leading zeros, use exact match, require explicit resolution of ambiguous aliases. Unknown codes prompt registration or association; no automatic record creation or stock changes.
- Ordinary merchandise uses identical labels per sellable SKU/variant; a scan counts a unit in a receiving or selling draft. A repeated scan is not automatically fraud or a duplicate item. Individual tracking is opt-in for equipment that genuinely requires serial/warranty handling. Never allow a generic product code to receive or sell an individually tracked item without specifying the unit.
- Product/variant identity can exist at zero stock. Individual unit identity may be prepared before receipt but is ineligible for sale until received. Reprinting retains the same token; revoke only when necessary and never silently remap an old token to another target. A cancelled unreceived unit cannot be activated through that purchase.

## Staff workflow
1. Add or select a product in a purchase order.
2. Choose Print labels and quantity (default outstanding ordered quantity). Ordinary product labels reuse their permanent QR. For individually tracked equipment, prepare distinct pending unit IDs.
3. On delivery, open Receive, scan or enter quantity, review and confirm. Explicit purchase origin; a non-purchase adjustment is a separate intentional action.
4. On sale, scan into the existing POS. For repair devices, scan into the existing Revio ticket. Show only relevant authorized actions.

No separate identity-management screen for ordinary staff. Keep manual search/entry available. Do not add batch/expiry, location tracking or warehouse workflows in this milestone. No changes to approved storefront or repair screens beyond necessary integrations.

## Reliability seam
Resolver is read-only with respect to business records. Every mutation passes through authorized domain commands and checks identity type, eligibility, quantities, current versions and idempotency. Printing never updates stock. Receiving a serialized unit, activating it, and recording its receipt/stock effect must be consistent; use the existing transaction boundary when possible. If records live in separate stores, define recovery and pending states before enabling operations; no claim of cross-database atomicity. Do not acknowledge success until durable outcome is known. Offline capture can be a draft only; no offline stock confirmation in the initial release.

## Next implementation gate
A focused prototype change first: consolidate resolution, preserve product/repair label mappings, enforce simulated role and serialized tracking checks at the stock command seam, and keep normal staff UX unchanged. Add label preparation only after those checks pass. Production requires authoritative cross-device persistence, authorization and transaction/recovery tests; browser localStorage is not that authority.

Acceptance: unchanged label after rename/SKU edit; aliases cannot steal mappings; unknown/revoked labels fail closed; repeat print has zero stock effect; pending unit cannot be sold; same unit cannot be received/sold twice; ordinary repeated product scans count correctly; wrong role cannot mutate stock; purchase receipt replay changes stock once; cancellation preserves prior receipts; no private repair information from public scans; old printed labels continue resolving. Physical printer/scanner tests and actual Take App staff parity remain separate gates.

Status: design proposal recorded only. V20.1 audit blockers remain open until tests verify implementation. No production code, databases, or deployment changed.
